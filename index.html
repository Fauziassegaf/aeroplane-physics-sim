Equations of Uniformly Accelerated Motion (SUVAT) Live numbers dynamically substituted from your current simulation parameters
1. Velocity-Time Relation (Finding Takeoff Time t)
v = u + at &implies; t = &frac{v - u}{a}

Starting from rest (u = 0), required takeoff time is directly proportional to target speed and inversely proportional to acceleration:

t = (75.0 - 0) / 2.2 = 34.09 s
2. Position-Time Relation (Runway Distance s)
s = ut + ½at² &implies; s = ½at²

Because the aeroplane starts from rest, distance is quadratic with time. If time doubles, distance quadruples:

s = 0 + ½(2.2)(34.09)² = 1,278.4 m
3. Torricelli's Equation (Independent of Time)
v² = u² + 2as &implies; s = &frac{v² - u²}{2a} = &frac{v²}{2a}

Crucial engineering formula: Doubling takeoff speed requires 4× the runway distance at the same engine acceleration!

s = (75.0)² / (2 × 2.2) = 1,278.4 m
4. Average Velocity & Geometric Meaning
vavg = &frac{u + v}{2} = &frac{v}{2},   s = vavg × t

In linear acceleration, average velocity is exactly half of the final takeoff speed. Distance corresponds directly to the triangular area under the v-t graph:

v_avg = 37.5 m/s &implies; s = 37.5 × 34.09 = 1,278.4 m

✈️ Aeronautical Engineering Context: V1, Vr, and V2

In real commercial aviation, three critical speeds govern takeoff along a runway:
• V1 (Decision Speed): The maximum speed at which the pilots can abort the takeoff and safely stop on the remaining runway.
• Vr (Rotation Speed): The target velocity modeled in this simulator where the pilot pulls back on the control column to pitch the nose up.
• V2 (Takeoff Safety Speed): The minimum climb speed that ensures obstacle clearance even if one engine fails after liftoff.
